UAV Launcher — Bungee Strand Selection

This section explains how the initial bungee strand count was selected for the launcher and how flight data was used to refine the configuration for a heavier 4 kg airframe.

Known parameters
Rail length: 3.0 m
Powered stroke: 2.0 m
Single-strand force: 96 N (peak @ ~90 % stretch)
Prototype launch mass: 2.3 kg
Heavy airframe launch mass: 4.0 kg

1. Initial choice of four bungee strands

The launcher uses a 3 m rail with a 2 m powered stroke. A single length of bungee was cut so that a full 2 m draw corresponded to approximately 90 % stretch when routed from the launcher base, around the nose pulley, and back to the dolly.

A pull test with a baggage scale showed that one strand produced a peak draw force of:

\[ F_\text{strand} \approx 9.8~\text{kgf} \approx 96~\text{N} \]

The first airframe used for launcher development had a known mass of:

\[ m_\text{proto} = 2.3~\text{kg} \]

The performance target for this aircraft was a launch velocity close to its cruise requirement:

\[ v_\text{target} \approx 16~\text{m/s} \]

The corresponding kinetic energy at launch is:

\[ KE_\text{target} = \tfrac{1}{2} m_\text{proto} v_\text{target}^2 = \tfrac{1}{2} \times 2.3 \times 16^2 \approx 294~\text{J} \]

Assumption (sizing model): the bungee is approximated as a constant-force element over the 2 m stroke. In practice, force varies with elongation and efficiency is reduced by pulley friction and rail losses.

To obtain a first-order estimate of launcher capability, the bungee was modelled as providing approximately constant force over the 2 m stroke. For a single strand this gives an ideal work input of:

\[ W_\text{1 strand} \approx F_\text{strand} \cdot s = 96 \times 2 = 192~\text{J} \]

Even before allowing for real-world inefficiencies, a single strand does not meet the 294 J requirement for the prototype. Two strands are theoretically sufficient in the ideal model, but offer little margin once friction, non-linear elastic behaviour, and other losses are included.

To ensure adequate system stiffness, safety margin and reliable acceleration, the launcher was therefore configured with:

\[ N = 4~\text{strands} \]

Selected configuration for prototype:
4 bungee strands for a 2.3 kg airframe

This configuration provided sufficient elastic energy and a comfortable performance margin, which was validated during early flight testing.

2. Refinement using 4 kg flight data

As the project progressed, the same launcher was used with a heavier airframe at a launch mass of:

\[ m_1 = 4~\text{kg} \]

With four strands installed, the launcher exit speed was measured using a slow-motion timing rig:

\[ v_1 \approx 12.2~\text{m/s} \]

The corresponding launch energy is:

\[ KE_1 = \tfrac{1}{2} m_1 v_1^2 = \tfrac{1}{2} \times 4 \times 12.2^2 \approx 298~\text{J} \]

From this result, an empirical linear scaling factor can be inferred for this launcher geometry, representing an effective speed contribution per strand:

\[ v_\text{per strand} \approx \frac{v_1}{N_1} = \frac{12.2}{4} \approx 3.05~\text{m/s per strand} \]

The objective for the 4 kg configuration was to achieve a launch speed comfortably greater than 16 m/s to improve climb-out margin and ensure consistent performance. Using the measured strand scaling factor, the required strand count can be estimated as:

\[ N_2 \approx \frac{v_\text{target}}{v_\text{per strand}} = \frac{16}{3.05} \approx 5.2 \]

Rounding up and adding a safety overhead, the launcher was upgraded from four to:

\[ N_2 = 6~\text{strands} \]

Final heavy-airframe configuration:
6 bungee strands for a 4.0 kg airframe

Subsequent tests with the 4 kg airframe and six strands produced measured launch speeds in excess of 60 km/h (\(\approx 16.7~\text{m/s}\)), confirming that the additional strands delivered the desired performance margin.

3. Design conclusion

For this launcher geometry and bungee stock, strand count and launch speed scale approximately linearly over the 2.3–4 kg range. An empirical sizing rule of

\[ v_\text{per strand} \approx 3~\text{m/s} \]

provides a useful baseline for future configurations, with at least one strand of additional margin recommended for operational robustness and variations in bungee condition.
